Turn a 10x Genomics feature-barcode count matrix into quality-filtered cells, principal components, graph-based clusters, and a UMAP embedding using parallel Seurat (R) and Scanpy (Python) workflows on the public PBMC 3k dataset.

Format: Hands-on lab. Run a full single-cell QC, normalization, PCA, clustering, and UMAP workflow on the public PBMC 3k matrix. Commands below stage the data locally and walk through parallel Seurat (R) and Scanpy (Python) scripts.

What problem this solves: Raw droplet single-cell count matrices mix intact cells with empty droplets, damaged cells leaking cytoplasmic RNA, and wide differences in sequencing depth. Clustering uncleaned counts groups technical artifacts instead of cell types.

What you will be able to do:

  • Stage and verify a 10x Genomics Matrix Exchange directory (matrix.mtx, barcodes.tsv, and genes.tsv or features.tsv.gz).
  • Compute per-cell quality control (QC) metrics (detected genes, UMI counts, mitochondrial RNA fraction) and inspect violin distributions before filtering.
  • Normalize counts, select 2,000 highly variable genes (HVGs), scale expression, and choose principal components (PCs) from an elbow plot.
  • Construct a nearest-neighbor graph, run Louvain or Leiden clustering, visualize embeddings with UMAP, and check clusters against canonical PBMC marker genes.

Prerequisites & time: About 45-60 minutes. Assumes basic shell commands (Command Line Part 1), introductory R or Python, and familiarity with count matrices (Introduction to Bioinformatics).

Scope: Uses the public 10x Genomics PBMC 3k filtered matrix (2,700 input barcodes) with Seurat v4/v5 in R as the primary executed route and Scanpy in Python as a parallel route. Example thresholds (200-2,500 genes and <5% mitochondrial counts) are teaching starting points for PBMCs, calibrated per tissue and protocol on real studies.

Why each stage exists before you run the code

In droplet single-cell RNA-seq, each droplet is tagged with a cell barcode (identifying the droplet) and each captured transcript carries a unique molecular identifier (UMI) (a random tag that collapses PCR duplicates so each original mRNA molecule counts once). Three QC covariates capture most library failures:

  • Detected genes per barcode (nFeature_RNA in Seurat; n_genes_by_counts in Scanpy): very low counts flag empty droplets or degraded fragments, while unusually high counts can flag doublets (two cells captured in one droplet).
  • Total UMI counts per barcode (nCount_RNA in Seurat; total_counts in Scanpy): measures sequencing depth and transcript capture per cell; scales strongly with detected genes.
  • Mitochondrial transcript percentage (percent.mt in Seurat; pct_counts_mt in Scanpy): when a cell membrane ruptures during dissociation, cytoplasmic mRNA leaks out while mitochondrial transcripts remain enclosed, raising the fraction of reads mapping to MT- genes.

Why the pipeline follows this exact order:

  1. Cell QC before normalization: Removing broken droplets first prevents outlier cells from distorting gene-variance estimates during HVG selection.
  2. Log-normalization (scale factor 10,000): Divides each cell's gene counts by its total UMI count, multiplies by 10,000 (counts per 10,000), and takes log(1 + x) so differences in sequencing depth do not dominate distances between cells.
  3. Selecting 2,000 HVGs (and saving unscaled expression): Most of the ~33,000 annotated features are zero or invariant across cells. Restricting PCA to the 2,000 genes with the highest cell-to-cell variance concentrates biological signal and reduces memory, while keeping adata.raw (Scanpy) or the full RNA assay (Seurat) preserves all genes for later marker plots.
  4. Scaling and PCA: Zero-centering and unit-variance scaling puts highly and moderately expressed HVGs on a comparable scale before PCA summarizes correlated gene programs (such as T-cell or monocyte modules) into 50 orthogonal principal components.
  5. Neighbor graph, community detection, and UMAP: FindNeighbors / sc.pp.neighbors builds a k-nearest-neighbor graph in the selected PC space (here PCs 1-10), Louvain or Leiden partitions that graph into discrete communities, and UMAP projects the same neighborhood structure into two dimensions for inspection.

Required files and workspace

The official Seurat PBMC 3k tutorial uses a public 10x Genomics PBMC archive. From your project root, download and stage its legacy matrix.mtx, barcodes.tsv, and genes.tsv files under data/pbmc3k/filtered_feature_bc_matrix (the archive extracts under filtered_gene_bc_matrices/hg19/):

mkdir -p data/pbmc3k
curl -L --fail --output /tmp/pbmc3k_filtered.tar.gz https://cf.10xgenomics.com/samples/cell/pbmc3k/pbmc3k_filtered_gene_bc_matrices.tar.gz
tar -xzf /tmp/pbmc3k_filtered.tar.gz -C data/pbmc3k
mv data/pbmc3k/filtered_gene_bc_matrices/hg19 data/pbmc3k/filtered_feature_bc_matrix
ls -lh data/pbmc3k/filtered_feature_bc_matrix

Check your staged directory: ls -lh data/pbmc3k/filtered_feature_bc_matrix should show three non-empty files: barcodes.tsv, genes.tsv, and matrix.mtx. Cell Ranger v2 exports uncompressed genes.tsv, whereas Cell Ranger v3+ exports compressed barcodes.tsv.gz, features.tsv.gz, and matrix.mtx.gz. Keep the native filenames; both Seurat Read10X and Scanpy read_10x_mtx auto-detect both layouts. On multimodal matrices (such as CITE-seq), Read10X() returns a named list where you pass pbmc.data[["Gene Expression"]] to CreateSeuratObject(), while Scanpy uses gex_only=True.

  • data/pbmc3k/filtered_feature_bc_matrix/: staged 10x Matrix Exchange input.
  • results/pbmc_seurat.rds and results/figures/*.png: outputs created by the R/Seurat script.
  • results/pbmc_scanpy.h5ad and results/figures/*.png: outputs created by the optional Python/Scanpy script.
Optional: software prerequisites and recording versions

The main route uses R with Seurat (v4 or v5) and ggplot2; running Leiden clustering in Seurat v5 (algorithm = 4) also requires the leidenbase R package. For the optional Python route, Scanpy requires scikit-misc for flavor="seurat_v3" and igraph plus leidenalg for sc.tl.leiden. Record your exact environment with sessionInfo() in R or python -c "import scanpy; print(scanpy.__version__)" in Python (see Conda & Mamba Part 1 for isolated environments).

Reproducible sequential workflow

Seurat (R) workflow

# R script: scripts/01_seurat_qc_clustering.R
# Input: data/pbmc3k/filtered_feature_bc_matrix/
# Output: results/pbmc_seurat.rds and figures saved to results/figures/

library(Seurat)          # v4/5

dir.create("results/figures", recursive = TRUE, showWarnings = FALSE)

# 1) Load 10x data
pbmc.data <- Read10X(data.dir = "data/pbmc3k/filtered_feature_bc_matrix/")
pbmc <- CreateSeuratObject(counts = pbmc.data, project = "pbmc3k", min.cells = 3, min.features = 200)

# 2) Compute percent mitochondrial reads (human: genes starting with MT-)
pbmc[["percent.mt"]] <- PercentageFeatureSet(pbmc, pattern = "^MT-")

# 3) Visualize QC metrics and choose thresholds
png("results/figures/qc_violin.png", width=1200, height=400)
VlnPlot(pbmc, features = c("nFeature_RNA", "nCount_RNA", "percent.mt"), ncol = 3)
dev.off()

# 4) Example filtering (adjust thresholds to your experiment)
message("Cells before QC filters: ", ncol(pbmc))
pbmc <- subset(pbmc, subset = nFeature_RNA > 200 & nFeature_RNA < 2500)
message("After gene-count filters: ", ncol(pbmc))
pbmc <- subset(pbmc, subset = percent.mt < 5)
message("After mitochondrial filter: ", ncol(pbmc))

# 5) Normalize, find variable features, scale
pbmc <- NormalizeData(pbmc, normalization.method = "LogNormalize", scale.factor = 10000)
pbmc <- FindVariableFeatures(pbmc, selection.method = "vst", nfeatures = 2000)
pbmc <- ScaleData(pbmc, features = VariableFeatures(pbmc))

# 6) PCA
pbmc <- RunPCA(pbmc, features = VariableFeatures(pbmc))
png("results/figures/pca_elbow.png", width=800, height=600)
ElbowPlot(pbmc, ndims = 50)
dev.off()

# 7) Neighbors, clustering, UMAP (replace with dimensions chosen after PCA inspection)
pcs <- 1:10
pbmc <- FindNeighbors(pbmc, dims = pcs)
pbmc <- FindClusters(pbmc, resolution = 0.5)   # Louvain default; Leiden (algorithm = 4) needs its documented dependency
pbmc <- RunUMAP(pbmc, dims = pcs)
png("results/figures/umap_clusters.png", width=800, height=600)
DimPlot(pbmc, reduction = "umap", label = TRUE)
dev.off()

# 8) Save object
saveRDS(pbmc, file = "results/pbmc_seurat.rds")

Save this script as scripts/01_seurat_qc_clustering.R and run it from the project root with Rscript scripts/01_seurat_qc_clustering.R. In Seurat v5, expression matrices live inside Assay5 layers (counts, data, and scale.data, inspected with Layers(pbmc) or LayerData()); all functions above work identically across Seurat v4 and v5.

Key outputs and what each step controls:

  • min.cells = 3, min.features = 200 drops genes present in fewer than 3 cells and barcodes with fewer than 200 detected genes; VlnPlot saves results/figures/qc_violin.png so you can verify the 200-2,500 gene and <5% mitochondrial cutoffs visually.
  • NormalizeData, FindVariableFeatures(selection.method = "vst", nfeatures = 2000), and ScaleData standardize library depth and isolate the 2,000 most variable genes for PCA without modifying raw counts.
  • ElbowPlot saves results/figures/pca_elbow.png across 50 PCs so you can adjust pcs <- 1:10 before building the SNN graph (FindNeighbors) and Louvain clusters (FindClusters, default algorithm = 1; pass algorithm = 4 for Leiden when leidenbase is installed).
  • DimPlot saves results/figures/umap_clusters.png and saveRDS writes results/pbmc_seurat.rds with cluster labels stored in seurat_clusters.

Scanpy (Python) workflow

# Python script: scripts/01_scanpy_qc_clustering.py
# Input: Cell Ranger filtered 10x MEX directory (gene-expression features)
# Output: results/pbmc_scanpy.h5ad and figures in results/figures/

import os
import scanpy as sc

os.makedirs("results/figures", exist_ok=True)
sc.settings.figdir = "results/figures"

# Read 10x MEX files; v3+ compressed filenames are expected by default.
adata = sc.read_10x_mtx(
    "data/pbmc3k/filtered_feature_bc_matrix/",
    var_names="gene_symbols",
    cache=True,
    gex_only=True,
)
adata.var_names_make_unique()

# Identify mitochondrial genes using the naming convention in this reference.
# Change the prefix/case rule if your feature annotation uses another convention.
adata.var["mt"] = adata.var_names.str.upper().str.startswith("MT-")
sc.pp.calculate_qc_metrics(adata, qc_vars=["mt"], percent_top=None, inplace=True)
sc.pl.violin(
    adata,
    ["total_counts", "n_genes_by_counts", "pct_counts_mt"],
    jitter=0.4,
    multi_panel=True,
    save="_qc_violin.png",
)

# Example filters only: inspect distributions and choose criteria for this sample.
print(f"Cells before QC filters: {adata.n_obs}")
adata = adata[adata.obs["n_genes_by_counts"] > 200].copy()
print(f"After lower gene-count filter: {adata.n_obs}")
adata = adata[adata.obs["n_genes_by_counts"] < 2500].copy()
print(f"After upper gene-count filter: {adata.n_obs}")
adata = adata[adata.obs["pct_counts_mt"] < 5].copy()
print(f"After mitochondrial filter: {adata.n_obs}")

# Preserve count values because seurat_v3 HVG selection expects counts.
adata.layers["counts"] = adata.X.copy()
sc.pp.normalize_total(adata, target_sum=1e4)
sc.pp.log1p(adata)
adata.raw = adata.copy()  # log-normalized expression before restricting to HVGs
sc.pp.highly_variable_genes(
    adata, n_top_genes=2000, flavor="seurat_v3", layer="counts"
)
adata = adata[:, adata.var.highly_variable].copy()
sc.pp.scale(adata, max_value=10)

# Inspect the variance plot, then use the same selected PC range below.
sc.tl.pca(adata, svd_solver="arpack")
sc.pl.pca_variance_ratio(adata, log=True, save="_pca_elbow.png")
sc.pp.neighbors(adata, n_neighbors=10, n_pcs=10)  # adjust n_pcs after inspection
sc.tl.leiden(adata, resolution=0.5)
sc.tl.umap(adata)
sc.pl.umap(adata, color=["leiden"], save="_umap_clusters.png")
adata.write("results/pbmc_scanpy.h5ad")

Save this script as scripts/01_scanpy_qc_clustering.py and run it with python3 scripts/01_scanpy_qc_clustering.py. In Scanpy 1.10+, calling sc.tl.leiden(adata, resolution=0.5) without a backend emits a FutureWarning because the default backend is shifting from leidenalg to igraph; for new scripts, pass flavor="igraph", n_iterations=2, directed=False to silence the warning and use the faster backend.

  • sc.pl.violin writes results/figures/violin_qc_violin.png (Scanpy prepends the plot type to save=) and prints cell counts after each filter step.
  • adata.layers["counts"] retains unnormalized counts for flavor="seurat_v3", while adata.raw = adata.copy() saves full log-normalized expression across all genes before slicing adata to 2,000 HVGs for sc.pp.scale.
  • sc.pl.pca_variance_ratio saves results/figures/pca_variance_ratio_pca_elbow.png so you can tune n_pcs before computing sc.pp.neighbors, sc.tl.leiden, and sc.tl.umap.
  • adata.write saves results/pbmc_scanpy.h5ad containing the scaled HVG matrix, adata.raw, layers["counts"], PC/UMAP coordinates in adata.obsm, and cluster assignments in adata.obs["leiden"].

Optional PBMC marker sanity check

Before assigning biological names, verify that your numeric clusters correspond to coherent immune gene programs using a canonical PBMC marker panel: B cells (MS4A1, CD79A), T cells (CD3D, IL7R), NK cells (NKG7), monocytes (LYZ, FCGR3A), and platelets (PPBP). Checking expression across clusters confirms the clustering reflects real biology rather than library depth alone.

library(Seurat)

pbmc <- readRDS("results/pbmc_seurat.rds")
if (!("RNA" %in% SeuratObject::Assays(pbmc))) {
  stop("The saved object has no RNA assay")
}
if (!("seurat_clusters" %in% colnames(pbmc[[]]))) {
  stop("The saved object has no seurat_clusters column")
}

candidates <- c("MS4A1", "CD79A", "CD3D", "IL7R",
                "NKG7", "LYZ", "FCGR3A", "PPBP")
features <- intersect(candidates, rownames(pbmc[["RNA"]]))
message("Candidates not present: ",
        paste(setdiff(candidates, features), collapse = ", "))

if (length(features)) {
  p <- DotPlot(pbmc, features = features, assay = "RNA",
               group.by = "seurat_clusters") + RotatedAxis()
  print(p)
  dir.create("results/figures", recursive = TRUE, showWarnings = FALSE)
  ggplot2::ggsave("results/figures/pbmc_marker_dotplot.png",
                  plot = p, width = 8, height = 5)
} else {
  message("No candidate genes are present; no plot made.")
}

Candidate dot plots are a sanity check rather than formal annotation; for full reference mapping and statistical marker testing, continue to Cell-Type Annotation Methods.

Decision guide: choosing parameters and methods

The PBMC 3k script uses simple defaults so every step is transparent. When you move to multi-sample studies or non-PBMC tissues, use this table to decide when to stick with the defaults and when to switch methods:

Decision Starter default Production / alternative When to switch
Cell QC cutoffs Fixed bounds (200-2,500 genes, <5% MT) Sample-stratified MAD outliers (scuttle::isOutlier) + doublet caller (scDblFinder / Scrublet) + ambient RNA removal (SoupX / CellBender) Switch for multi-batch studies, high-mito tissues (heart, kidney, liver, tumors), or libraries with >3,000 cells where doublets exceed ~2-3%.
Normalization LogNormalize (scale 1e4 + log1p) SCTransform (regularized negative binomial residuals, SCT v2 in Seurat) Use SCTransform when sequencing depth varies widely or when regressing covariates like percent.mt; stick with LogNormalize for >300,000 cells or non-UMI protocols like Smart-seq2.
PC count 1:10 1:15 to 1:30 Increase when the elbow plot decays gradually or when rare subclusters only separate at higher PCs; check PC gene loadings first.
Clustering algorithm & resolution Louvain (algorithm = 1, res = 0.5) or Scanpy Leiden Leiden (Seurat algorithm = 4 with leidenbase; Scanpy flavor="igraph", n_iterations=2, directed=False) Prefer Leiden on large graphs because it guarantees connected communities; use lower resolution (0.2-0.4) for major lineages and higher (0.8-1.2) to inspect fine subtypes.

Interpreting outputs and troubleshooting common errors

Before moving downstream, confirm that your clusters reflect cell biology rather than technical artifacts:

  • Color UMAP by QC covariates: Plot nCount_RNA, nFeature_RNA, and percent.mt (or total_counts, n_genes_by_counts, pct_counts_mt) on the UMAP. If an entire cluster is defined solely by low UMI counts or high mitochondrial fraction without distinct marker genes, inspect it as a candidate damaged-cell group.
  • Inspect doublet candidates carefully: A small cluster with high gene and UMI counts might be doublets (co-expressing mutually exclusive lineage markers such as CD3D and MS4A1) or a genuinely large, transcriptionally active population (such as cycling plasmablasts). Check marker co-expression before discarding it.
  • Check canonical PBMC markers: In healthy PBMCs, clusters typically align with naive/memory CD4+ T cells (IL7R, CCR7, S100A4), CD8+ T cells (CD8A), NK cells (GNLY, NKG7, low CD3D), B cells (MS4A1, CD79A), CD14+ monocytes (CD14, LYZ), FCGR3A+ monocytes (FCGR3A, MS4A7), dendritic cells (FCER1A, CST3), and platelets (PPBP).

Common errors and symptoms with concrete fixes:

  • Seurat error on multimodal 10x input (Read10X() returns a list):
    Cause: Datasets with antibody capture (CITE-seq) or CRISPR guides return a named list rather than a single matrix, so CreateSeuratObject(counts = pbmc.data) fails.
    Fix: Check names(pbmc.data) and pass the RNA matrix explicitly: CreateSeuratObject(counts = pbmc.data[["Gene Expression"]], ...).
  • percent.mt is 0 for every cell:
    Cause: Mouse gene symbols use lowercase mt- (not MT-), or your matrix uses Ensembl gene IDs (ENSG...) instead of gene symbols.
    Fix: Inspect head(rownames(pbmc)) (R) or adata.var_names[:10] (Python). Use pattern = "^mt-" for mouse symbols, or match against the chromosome/MT gene list from your GTF if using Ensembl IDs.
  • Scanpy ImportError for scikit-misc or igraph / leidenalg:
    Cause: flavor="seurat_v3" in sc.pp.highly_variable_genes needs scikit-misc, and sc.tl.leiden needs igraph and leidenalg.
    Fix: Install them in your active environment (pip install scikit-misc igraph leidenalg or via conda-forge). If sc.tl.leiden prints a FutureWarning about the default backend changing to igraph, pass flavor="igraph", n_iterations=2, directed=False.
  • Seurat v5 GetAssayData / slot deprecation errors when inspecting matrices:
    Cause: Seurat v5 stores counts and normalized data in layers (Assay5) rather than v4 slots.
    Fix: Use LayerData(pbmc, assay = "RNA", layer = "counts") or LayerData(pbmc, layer = "data") instead of slot = ....
  • Clusters driven by nCount_RNA or percent.mt instead of markers:
    Cause: Sequencing depth or cell-stress variation dominates the top PCs, or multiple samples have batch effects.
    Fix: For single-sample technical trends, pass vars.to.regress = c("nCount_RNA", "percent.mt") to ScaleData() or SCTransform(). For multi-sample batches, use dedicated integration (see scRNA-seq Integration Strategies).
  • No sharp elbow in PCA, or over-clustered UMAP:
    Cause: Complex tissues decay smoothly in variance, or resolution is too high for the sample size.
    Fix: Inspect top PC gene loadings with VizDimLoadings(pbmc, dims = 1:15) or DimHeatmap() (prefer these over Seurat's slow, legacy JackStraw test, which is incompatible with SCTransform), and test resolutions from 0.3 to 0.8 alongside marker dot plots.

Practice check: diagnose two QC scenarios

  1. Scenario A: In a 10,000-cell PBMC run, Cluster 7 (90 cells) has twice the median nFeature_RNA and nCount_RNA of all other clusters and expresses both CD3D (T-cell marker) and MS4A1 (B-cell marker). Should you keep it as a rare hybrid cell type or flag it?
  2. Scenario B: On a dissociated human kidney biopsy, 38% of cells have percent.mt between 6% and 14% with high nFeature_RNA and normal tubular marker expression. Should you apply the PBMC percent.mt < 5 cutoff?
Check your reasoning

Scenario A: Flag Cluster 7 as likely T-cell/B-cell doublets and confirm with a doublet caller (scDblFinder or Scrublet). High UMI and gene counts plus co-expression of mutually exclusive lineage markers is a classic heterotypic doublet signature, whereas cycling plasmablasts show high counts with B-cell/plasma markers (MZB1, JCHAIN, MKI67) rather than CD3D.

Scenario B: Do not blindly apply percent.mt < 5. Renal proximal tubule cells are mitochondria-rich; a strict 5% cutoff would discard viable epithelium. Plot nFeature_RNA against percent.mt per sample, distinguish damaged low-gene/high-MT cells from high-gene/moderate-MT tubular cells, and set a tissue-appropriate or MAD-based threshold.

Practical boundaries and version notes

  • Pin package versions: Seurat and Scanpy defaults evolve across major releases. Record sessionInfo() (R) or pip freeze (Python) alongside your analysis script.
  • Match mitochondrial patterns to your reference: Human symbols use ^MT-, mouse uses ^mt-, and Ensembl IDs require mapping against mitochondrial gene annotations.
  • UMAP reproducibility: UMAP is stochastic and version-sensitive. Record random seeds (Scanpy defaults to random_state=0; Seurat defaults to seed.use = 42 in RunUMAP) and do not expect identical coordinates across OSes or package versions.
  • Human data governance: Clinical or donor scRNA-seq matrices can carry identifiable metadata; follow IRB and data-use agreements before sharing objects.

Checklist before moving on

  1. Confirm data/pbmc3k/filtered_feature_bc_matrix/ holds valid 10x files and log your starting cell count.
  2. Inspect qc_violin.png before applying filters; confirm retained cell counts match expectations without silent zeroing.
  3. Verify pca_elbow.png and adjust PC count if major biological variation extends past PC 10.
  4. Check umap_clusters.png colored by both QC covariates and canonical markers before assigning biological labels.
  5. Archive software versions, random seeds, and input checksums alongside results/pbmc_seurat.rds or results/pbmc_scanpy.h5ad.

Author's lab note: When I move from PBMC benchmarks to real tumour biopsies or metabolically active tissues (such as kidney, myocardium, or hepatocytes), the classic 5% mitochondrial cutoff almost always throws away real biological populations. I plot nFeature_RNA against percent.mt separately for each library first, flag outliers with sample-stratified MAD bounds, and check whether high-mito clusters express coherent epithelial or stromal markers before removing a single cell.
